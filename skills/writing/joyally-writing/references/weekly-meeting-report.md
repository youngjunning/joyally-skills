# Weekly meeting report

Write for colleagues who need to understand business progress and decide what to do next. Lead each workstream with its current outcome or lifecycle state, then give the business value and the next relevant step. Use the reporting period the user requested, even when it differs from a calendar week.

Keep implementation details in the source project. Include a technical mechanism only when the meeting audience needs it to understand a decision, blocker, risk, or acceptance condition. A business update such as “该自动化功能已上线” can stand alone when that is the complete status the author wants to share; do not expand it into API changes, background jobs, tests, or release steps merely because those details are available.

Preserve evidence boundaries while editing for brevity. Distinguish shipped, deployed, verified, and planned states when they are material. Attribute user-provided outcomes and metrics without presenting them as independently measured. When the author corrects a status or removes a topic, revise the current report and its summary or next-step text consistently; preserve earlier reports as historical records unless asked to revise them.

Make every sentence change the meeting reader's understanding or next decision. Keep outcomes and lifecycle states, business value, material risks or blockers, and actionable next steps. Put supporting screenshots, attachment details, provenance, and measurement caveats in their captions, source, or notes; when a body sentence only repeats that evidence without changing the judgment, delete it.

Keep the meeting view short: each workstream normally gets one or two brief sentences covering the result or current state and the next decision or verifiable step. If a mechanism, secondary scenario list, attribution, or measurement boundary needs more space, move it to an expandable note or source caption. The main report should still name a material blocker or uncertainty; notes explain it rather than hide it.

Write next-period priorities as verifiable outcomes, not activity labels. Each retained item must name the concrete deliverable or decision and the condition that proves completion. Rewrite or omit generic statements built only from verbs such as “推进”, “跟踪”, “建设”, or “优化”; if no deliverable or acceptance condition is known, leave the item out rather than padding the section.

Use Markdown as the report prose source when the destination supports rich text. If a site field is plain text but the requested report needs emphasis, add a safe Markdown rendering path while keeping existing plain strings compatible. Bold only the decision layer: the exact business special-project or workstream name, a material lifecycle state, and a key metric that changes how the reader judges progress. Keep the number and unit together, such as `**成功率 99%**` or `**日均调用量 200**`. Leave ordinary technical terms, supporting counts, caveats, and sentence-ending punctuation unbolded; do not bold a whole sentence or paragraph.

Use italics for a short pending action, limitation, or estimate that needs a different reading from a confirmed result. When the destination has a designed accent color, reserve it for a small number of decision-relevant provisional figures or acceptance states; the design layer owns the color and contrast, so prose must remain understandable without color. Combined bold and italic Markdown can mark those phrases if the renderer maps it to the accent. Never color a whole paragraph, insert inline color HTML into report prose, or give every status the same emphasis. Check the rendered report in its actual theme, including dark mode when present.
